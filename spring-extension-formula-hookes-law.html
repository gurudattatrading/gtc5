Spring Extension Formula (Hooke's Law): x = F ÷ k, with Examples and Calculator

The extension of a spring is the force divided by the spring rate: x = F ÷ k. This page gives the formula, worked examples in N and mm and in lbf and inches, initial tension for extension springs, springs in series and parallel, the energy stored, and a free calculator.

The formula

For a spring that follows Hooke's law, the force and the extension are proportional:

F = k × x

  • F is the force on the spring (N or lbf)
  • k is the spring rate or spring constant (N/mm, N/m or lbf/in)
  • x is the extension, the change in length from the free length (mm, m or in)

Rearranged, the extension of a spring is x = F ÷ k, and the spring constant is k = F ÷ x. This holds while the spring is within its elastic limit, so it returns to its original length when the load is removed.

Extension springs with initial tension

A real extension spring is wound with its coils pressed together, so it does not begin to stretch until the pull is larger than the initial tension F₀. The formula becomes F = F₀ + k × x, and the extension is x = (F − F₀) ÷ k. For example, with F₀ = 5 N, k = 2 N/mm and F = 50 N, the extension is (50 − 5) ÷ 2 = 22.5 mm. Energy stored

The work done in stretching a spring from rest is ½ × k × x² (plus F₀ × x if there is initial tension). For k = 2 N/mm and x = 25 mm that is ½ × 2 × 25² = 625 N·mm, or 0.625 J.

Limits of the formula

  • It only works within the elastic limit. Stretch a spring too far and it takes a permanent set.
  • Compression springs are linear until they get close to solid height. Torsion springs follow torque = rate × angle. See our torsion spring calculator and compression spring calculator.
  • The spring rate itself comes from the wire diameter, coil diameter, number of coils and the material. Our extension spring calculator works it out from those.

Spring extension: FAQs

What is the formula for the extension of a spring?

Extension x equals force F divided by spring rate k, so x = F ÷ k. For a spring with initial tension F₀, x = (F − F₀) ÷ k.

How do I calculate the spring constant?

Divide the force by the extension: k = F ÷ x. If a spring stretches 40 mm under 8 N, k = 8 ÷ 40 = 0.2 N/mm, which is 200 N/m.

What is Hooke's law?

Hooke's law says that the force needed to stretch or compress a spring is proportional to the distance it is moved, F = k × x, as long as the spring stays within its elastic limit.

How do I combine springs in series and parallel?

In parallel the rates add, k = k₁ + k₂. In series the reciprocals add, 1/k = 1/k₁ + 1/k₂, which gives a softer combined spring.

What units should I use for spring rate?

N/mm, N/m or lbf/in. 1 N/mm equals 1,000 N/m and 5.710 lbf/in.
