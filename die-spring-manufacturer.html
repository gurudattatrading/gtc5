What is a die spring?

A die spring is a compression spring made from rectangular (or oval) wire instead of round wire. The flat wire section stores more energy for a given size, so a die spring gives a very high force in a small hole or over a small rod. That is why it is the standard spring for press tools, stamping and blanking dies, injection and plastic moulds, stripper plates and ejector systems.

Die spring duty levels

Duty levelWhat it means
Light dutyLowest spring rate for the size; used where little force is needed
Medium dutyThe most common general-purpose rating
Heavy dutyHigher force for the same hole and rod size
Extra-heavy dutyThe highest force, for compact, high-tonnage tooling

Die springs are colour-coded by duty, but the colour for each level differs between standards and manufacturers, so always match by duty level and dimensions, not colour alone. Our die spring colour code guide explains this.

How to select the right die spring

  • Hole diameter and rod diameter: the spring must fit in the hole or over the rod with a little clearance.
  • Free length: choose it so the spring is preloaded a little at rest and has enough stroke for your tool.
  • Duty level or working load: the force you need at the working stroke.
  • Stroke: every duty level has a recommended maximum deflection as a percentage of free length. For long life, keep your working stroke well below the maximum.

Fitting and care

Guide the spring on a rod or inside a hole so it cannot buckle sideways. Replace die springs in sets, not one at a time, so the load stays even across the tool. Replace a spring when its free length has visibly shortened, it is cracked or bent, or the tool no longer holds its load.

Materials
