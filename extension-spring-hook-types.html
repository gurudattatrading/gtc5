Extension Spring Hook Types: Chart, Uses and How to Choose

The hooks decide how an extension spring attaches and how long it lasts. This guide shows the main extension spring hook types in a chart, why hooks are where springs usually fail, and how to choose the right hook for your load and mounting.

The short answer

The common extension spring hook types are the machine hook (cross-over), side hook, extended hook, full loop, double loop and a threaded plug. A machine hook is the most economical and the usual choice. A full or double loop spreads the stress and lasts longer under repeated loads. An extended hook gives extra reach. Choose by the load, how often it repeats, and how the spring attaches.

Extension spring hook types chart

Hook typeDescriptionBest for
Machine hook (cross-over)Formed from the last coil, bent across the spring bodyThe standard, economical hook for general use
Side hook (side loop)The hook sits to one side of the coil bodyWhen the attachment point is offset
Extended hookA machine hook on a longer straight section of wireWhen extra reach to the mounting point is needed
Full loopA closed round loop in line with the coilHigher loads and repeated stretching, as it spreads the stress
Double loopA twisted double loop with two turns of wireHeavier, long-life duty and high loads
Threaded plug or insertA threaded end screwed into the coilAdjustable length and a firm mounting

Other special ends, such as swivel hooks and ends with a screw eye, can be made to your drawing.

Why hooks matter

The hook is where most extension springs fail. The bend where the hook leaves the coil concentrates stress, and every stretch flexes it. A sharp bend, a small hook radius or a damaged surface shortens the life. For repeated loading, ask for a full or double loop or a larger bend radius.

How to choose a hook

  1. Match the mounting. The hook must fit the hole, post or eye it attaches to. Measure that fitting.
  2. Think about the load. For heavy or repeating loads choose a full or double loop.
  3. Check the length. The type of hook changes the length inside hooks. Always state the length you need.
  4. Set the orientation. Say whether the two hooks are in line or turned at an angle, such as 90 degrees.
  5. Pick the right material and finish. Use galvanised or stainless steel where rust is a risk.

How to describe the hooks when you order

  • The hook type (machine hook, full loop, double loop and so on)
  • The hook opening and whether it is open or closed
  • The length inside hooks and the overall length
  • The angle between the hooks

Extension spring hooks: FAQs

What are the types of extension spring hooks?

The common types are the machine hook (cross-over), side hook, extended hook, full loop, double loop and threaded plug. Special ends can be made to a drawing.

Which extension spring hook is the strongest?

Full loops and double loops spread the stress and are generally stronger and longer lasting under repeated loads than a standard machine hook.

Why do extension spring hooks break?

The bend where the hook leaves the coil concentrates stress, so repeated stretching, a sharp bend, rust or overstretching can crack it.

How do I specify the hooks on an extension spring?

State the hook type, the opening, the length inside hooks and the angle between the hooks, or send a sample.

Can you make extension springs with special hooks?

Yes. We make machine hooks, side loops, extended hooks, full and double loops and special ends to your drawing or sample.
