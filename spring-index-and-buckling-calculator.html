What this calculator checks

Before you order a compression spring, three quick checks avoid most design mistakes: the spring index (is it easy to coil), the solid height (how far can it travel) and buckling (will a long spring bow sideways). Enter your spring below.

Compression spring index, solid height and buckling check (round wire, dimensions in mm)

The formulas

QuantityFormula
Spring indexC = D ÷ d (mean coil diameter divided by wire diameter). A value of about 4 to 12 is practical
Total coilsClosed ends: active coils + 2. Plain ends: active coils
Solid heightClosed and ground: total coils × d. Closed, not ground: (total coils + 1) × d
Buckling limitThe spring is stable if L0 is at most 2.63 × D ÷ α, where α is 0.5 between flat parallel plates, 0.707 with one end pivoted and 1 with both ends pivoted

Worked example

A spring with 2 mm wire, 18 mm mean diameter, 8 active coils and 50 mm free length, with closed and ground ends between flat plates, has a spring index of 9, total coils of 10 and a solid height of 20 mm, so it can travel 30 mm. The buckling limit is 2.63 × 18 ÷ 0.5 = 94.7 mm, so a 50 mm spring is stable. What to do if a check fails

  • Index too low: increase the coil diameter or reduce the wire diameter.
  • Index too high: use thicker wire or a smaller coil diameter, or add a guide.
  • Buckling risk: guide the spring on a rod or in a bore, shorten it, or ask for a barrel or conical shape.
  • Too close to solid: increase the free length or reduce the stroke.

Spring index and buckling: FAQs

What is a good spring index?

A spring index (mean coil diameter divided by wire diameter) of about 4 to 12 is practical. Very low values are hard to coil and very high values tangle or buckle.

How do I calculate the solid height of a compression spring?

For closed and ground ends, solid height is the total coils times the wire diameter. For closed ends that are not ground, use total coils plus one, times the wire diameter.

When will a compression spring buckle?

A spring is stable if its free length is at most 2.63 times the mean coil diameter divided by the end constant α, which is 0.5 between flat plates. Longer springs need a guide.

How close to solid height can a spring work?

Leave a clearance of at least 10 to 15 percent of the travel to solid, because working a spring down to solid shortens its life.

Can you make a spring if my design fails a check?

Yes. We suggest changes such as a different wire size, coil diameter, shape or a guide, and make the spring to your requirement.
