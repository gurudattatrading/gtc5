What is an extension spring?

An extension spring is a close-wound helical spring with a hook or loop at each end. It resists being pulled apart and returns to its original length when the load is released. Unlike a compression spring, its coils touch at rest and are held together by initial tension, so the spring does not start to stretch until the pull exceeds that force.

Extension springs are used wherever a pulling force or a return motion is needed — trampolines, garage and sliding-gate mechanisms, balancers, machine return mechanisms, tool and fixture clamps, and agricultural and automotive linkages.

How an extension spring works

The load at any extension x is F = Fi + k × x, where Fi is the initial tension and k is the spring rate, found with the same formula as for a compression spring: k = G · d⁴ / (8 · D³ · Na). Initial tension is wound into the spring while it is coiled and is not part of the rate. Once the applied load passes Fi, the spring stretches by x = (F − Fi) ÷ k. Design points that matter

  • Hooks usually govern. The hooks often carry the highest stress, so the body is not always the limiting part. Our calculator checks body shear, hook bending and hook torsion and reports the lowest as the safe load.
  • Specify length inside hooks. Give the length inside the hooks (or overall length), not only the body length.
  • State the hook orientation. Say whether the hooks are in line or turned to a set angle.
  • Limit the stretch. Fit a stop so the spring cannot be extended beyond its safe extension.
  • Mention the environment. Outdoor, wet or chemical use points to stainless steel or a protective finish.

Materials and finishes

Extension springs are made from spring steel wire such as music wire or hard drawn wire, or from stainless steel where corrosion resistance matters. Plated or galvanised finishes are common for outdoor use. See our material comparison and finishes and coatings guide.

Extension spring FAQs

What is the difference between an extension spring and a compression spring?

A compression spring is open-coiled and resists being squeezed. An extension spring is close-wound with a hook or loop at each end and resists being pulled apart.

What is initial tension in an extension spring?

Initial tension is the force that holds the coils together when the spring is at rest. The spring does not begin to stretch until the applied load exceeds it.

Why do extension springs usually fail at the hooks?

The bends where the hook meets the body concentrate stress, so overstretching or repeated cycling commonly cracks the spring there. A hook stress check is part of a good design.
