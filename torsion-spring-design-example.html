Torsion Spring Design Example: Step-by-Step Calculation with Calculator

How do you design a torsion spring? This page works through a full example, from the rate per degree and the torque at an angle to the bending stress and the rod size, with every formula in N·mm and a free calculator you can use for your own numbers.

The formulas

For a round-wire torsion spring with N active turns, wire diameter d, mean coil diameter D and modulus of elasticity E:

QuantityFormula
Rate per degreek = E × d⁴ ÷ (3667 × D × N) (N·mm per degree)
Torque at angle θ (degrees)T = k × θ
Spring indexC = D ÷ d
Stress correction factorKi = (4C² − C − 1) ÷ (4C × (C − 1))
Bending stressσ = 32 × T × Ki ÷ (π × d³)
Mean diameter after windingD′ = N × D ÷ (N + θ ÷ 360)

The 3667 comes from converting the rate per turn to a rate per degree. Use about 207,000 MPa for music wire and oil-tempered steel, and about 193,000 MPa for stainless steel 302/304.

Worked example

Design a torsion spring from 2.0 mm music wire, 16 mm mean coil diameter, 6 active turns, working at 90 degrees.

  1. Rate per degree: k = 207,000 × 2⁴ ÷ (3667 × 16 × 6) = 9.41 N·mm per degree.
  2. Torque at 90 degrees: T = 9.41 × 90 = 847 N·mm.
  3. Spring index: C = 16 ÷ 2 = 8, and Ki = (4 × 64 − 8 − 1) ÷ (4 × 8 × 7) = 1.103.
  4. Stress: σ = 32 × 847 × 1.103 ÷ (π × 8) = about 1,189 MPa. Compare it with the wire's tensile strength: as a guide, keep the stress below roughly 70 to 75 percent of it for a static load, and lower for repeated cycles.
  5. Diameter after winding: D′ = 6 × 16 ÷ (6 + 0.25) = 15.36 mm, so the inside diameter is 13.36 mm. The supporting rod must be smaller than this, for example 12 mm.

Design notes that save time

  • Active turns include the legs. Add the effect of the legs to the body turns: N = body turns + (L1 + L2) ÷ (3π × D), where L1 and L2 are the leg lengths.
  • Wind in the right direction. Load the spring in the direction that winds the coil tighter, and say left or right hand when you order.
  • Use a generous bend radius where the legs leave the coil, because stress concentrates there.
  • Check the rod and the housing at the working angle, not just the free diameter.

Torsion spring design: FAQs

How do I calculate the torque of a torsion spring?

First find the rate per degree, k = E × d⁴ ÷ (3667 × D × N), then multiply by the working angle in degrees: T = k × θ.

What is the spring rate formula for a torsion spring?

In N·mm per degree for round wire, k = E × d⁴ ÷ (3667 × D × N), where E is the modulus, d the wire diameter, D the mean coil diameter and N the active turns.

How do I find the stress in a torsion spring?

Use σ = 32 × T × Ki ÷ (π × d³), where Ki = (4C² − C − 1) ÷ (4C × (C − 1)) and C is the spring index D ÷ d.

What size rod should a torsion spring work on?

Smaller than the inside diameter after winding, which is N × D ÷ (N + θ ÷ 360) minus the wire diameter, so the rod does not bind.

Can you make a torsion spring from my design?

Yes. Send the wire size, diameter, turns, legs, direction of wind and the torque at an angle, or a sample, and we will quote.
